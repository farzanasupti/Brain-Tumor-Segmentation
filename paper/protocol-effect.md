# Protocol, not progress: two unstated reporting choices account for a 6.9-point Dice gap on a standard brain-tumour benchmark

**Status:** draft, 2026-09-19. Numbers are final; citations marked `[CITE]` are
the only blocking gap. Figures are generated by `python leakage.py --out figures/`
and every number below is reproducible from committed run artefacts without a GPU.

---

## Abstract

Segmentation results on the Cheng T1-CE brain-tumour dataset are widely reported
around 84 Dice, but the two choices that most affect that number — how the data
is split, and how per-slice scores are aggregated — are rarely stated. We train a
single U-Net under a pre-registered patient-disjoint five-fold protocol and reach
**0.7719 ± 0.0164** slice-averaged Dice, 6.9 points below a representative
published figure of 84.1 `[CITE]`. We then re-measure the same model, the same
folds and the same data under the two conventions a published number plausibly
uses. Pooled (dataset-level) aggregation is worth **+1.3 points**; a slice-level
split, which places slices from the same patient on both sides, is worth a further
**+5.1 points**. Together they reach **0.8358 ± 0.0054**, within **0.5 points** of
the published figure. No architectural difference is needed to explain the gap.

Two findings go beyond the mean shift. First, the leak **collapses between-fold
variance** by 3–8× depending on convention: a slice-level split stops sampling
patients, so the standard deviation reported beside a cross-validated mean no
longer measures patient-level variation. This holds whichever aggregation a paper
uses. Second, the effect is **concentrated in glioma** (+0.0717, zero-Dice slices
9.3% → 6.2%), the multi-slice, most variable class, while meningioma and pituitary
— already near their ceiling — move by less than 0.02. We argue that under these
conditions a single published scalar cannot serve as a comparison target, and
report what a like-for-like target would require.

## 1. Introduction

The Cheng dataset (3064 T1-CE slices, 233 patients; meningioma 708, glioma 1426,
pituitary 930) is a standard testbed for brain-tumour segmentation, and every
slice carries a tumour-type label. Its slice-level organisation makes one mistake
easy: a split drawn over slices rather than patients puts neighbouring slices of
the same tumour on both sides of the evaluation boundary. Neighbouring slices of
one tumour are nearly the same image.

We did not set out to study this. We pre-registered a conditioning experiment
with a **baseline credibility gate**: before testing any hypothesis, our
unconditioned U-Net had to land within about 1 Dice point of the published figure
under the same protocol. It missed by 6.9 points. This paper is the investigation
that gate forced, and the result is that the gap is not in the model.

**Contributions.**

1. A like-for-like measurement, same model and folds throughout, of what each
   reporting convention is worth on this benchmark: **+1.3** points for pooled
   aggregation, **+5.1** for a slice-level split, **0.5** residual.
2. The observation that leakage **collapses between-fold variance**, so a leaking
   cross-validated error bar does not measure what it is reported to measure —
   a claim independent of the aggregation convention.
3. A per-class decomposition showing the gain is almost entirely glioma, which
   explains *why* the leak pays and predicts where it will pay on other datasets.
4. A negative control: input resolution, the obvious alternative explanation for
   losses on small tumours, accounts for none of the gap.

## 2. Protocol

**Data.** Cheng et al., figshare 1512427 v5 (CC BY 4.0) `[CITE]`. 3064 slices,
233 patients.

**Registered protocol.** Stratified group five-fold at patient ID, seed 42,
committed before any run. Validation is carved from the training folds as whole
patients (70/10/20). Input 192 × 192, micro-batch 8, effective batch 16 by
accumulation, AdamW at 1e-4, Dice+BCE, maximum 150 epochs, early stopping on
validation Dice with patience 20. Model initialisation is seeded per fold.

**Leaking protocol.** Identical in every respect except that the five-fold split
is drawn over slices instead of patients. 224 of 233 patients then span more than
one fold, and 194 of 196 test patients are also seen in training. This is not a
strawman: it is what a naive `train_test_split` over a slice-indexed dataset
produces, and it is our best reconstruction of how figures on this dataset are
commonly obtained.

**Aggregation.** *Slice-averaged* Dice is the mean of per-slice scores.
*Pooled* (dataset-level) Dice is `2·ΣI / Σ(P+T)` over all test slices, which is
exactly the per-slice mean weighted by tumour size. *Patient-averaged* Dice
averages within patient, then across patients. Under a slice-level split every
slice is its own pseudo-patient, so patient-averaged collapses onto
slice-averaged — a consistency check on the split rather than a result.

**Scope.** One architecture, one dataset, one seed per fold. We are measuring
protocol, not proposing a method, so a stronger model would not change the
argument; the conventions apply to whatever is trained under them.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Both conventions, both splits

*(Figure: `figures/protocol.png` — per-fold pooled Dice under both protocols.)*

| convention | patient-disjoint (registered) | slice-level (leaking) | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| slice-averaged | 0.7719 ± 0.0164 | 0.8152 ± 0.0035 | +0.0433 |
| pooled | 0.7846 ± 0.0165 | 0.8358 ± 0.0054 | +0.0511 |
| patient-averaged | 0.7699 ± 0.0286 | 0.8152 ± 0.0035 | +0.0453 |

Five folds each side. The comparison is **not paired**: a slice-level split
reshuffles, so each fold's test set is a near-identical but not identical set of
slices. We therefore report differences of means and no paired test.

### 3.2 The gap, decomposed

| step | Dice | attributable |
|---|---|---|
| registered: patient-disjoint, slice-averaged | 0.7719 | — |
| + pooled aggregation | 0.7846 | +0.0127 |
| + slice-level split | 0.8358 | +0.0511 |
| published target `[CITE]` | 0.8410 | +0.0052 |

Taking the choices in the other order moves a little between the two middle rows
without changing the total or the 0.5-point residual. **Neither convention alone
comes close; together they account for 93% of a 6.9-point gap.**

### 3.3 Leakage collapses between-fold variance

| convention | sd, patient-disjoint | sd, slice-level | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| slice-averaged | 0.0164 | 0.0035 | 4.8× |
| pooled | 0.0165 | 0.0054 | 3.0× |
| patient-averaged | 0.0286 | 0.0035 | 8.3× |

This is the finding we consider most consequential and the one least visible in
a results table. A cross-validated mean is normally reported as `μ ± σ`, and σ is
read as *how much this result would move on a different sample of patients*.
Under a slice-level split the folds no longer sample patients — every patient is
in every fold — so σ measures only which slices of the same tumours landed where.
It shrinks by 3–8×, and a method comparison judged against it will find
differences significant that a patient-level protocol would not. The mean shift
can be argued about on the grounds of which convention a paper meant; this cannot.

### 3.4 Where the gain lands

*(Figure: `figures/protocol_by_type.png`.)*

| tumour type | patient-disjoint | slice-level | difference | zero-Dice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| glioma (n=1426) | 0.6627 | 0.7344 | +0.0717 | 9.3% → 6.2% |
| meningioma (n=708) | 0.8940 | 0.9139 | +0.0199 | 1.4% → 1.0% |
| pituitary (n=930) | 0.8464 | 0.8638 | +0.0174 | 0.5% → 0.5% |

The leak pays where cases are multi-slice, irregular and variable in appearance —
gliomas — and pays almost nothing on the two classes already near their ceiling.
Most of the effect is the recovery of complete failures: glioma slices scoring
exactly zero fall from 133 to 88 of 1426. This predicts that the size of a
leakage effect on another dataset is governed by how much within-patient
appearance variation the hard class has, not by dataset size.

### 3.5 Controls

**Resolution (negative).** Small tumours are the obvious alternative explanation
for glioma failures at 192 × 192. Training fold 0 at 256 × 256 scored 0.7901
against 0.7914 — a difference of −0.0013 on the same 613 test slices — and small
gliomas got *worse* (−0.0287, zero-Dice 19 → 27 of 143). Resolution explains none
of the gap.

**Extra training (not supported).** The leak reaches validation as well as test,
so the leaking arm can stop later. Across the five folds gain and epoch count are
weakly negatively correlated (r = −0.21, n = 5): the fold that trained longest
(108 epochs) gained least (+0.0207), and the fold that stopped soonest (20 epochs,
against that baseline fold's 60) gained most (+0.0638). At n = 5 this rules the
explanation out only loosely, and the two extreme folds carry it; what can be said
is that gain does not increase with training. *(Figure: `figures/protocol_epochs.png`.)*

**One fold is not enough.** We first measured the split effect on fold 0 alone and
recorded a prediction, before running folds 1–4, that the five-fold leaking figure
would be near 0.805 pooled. It was 0.8358. Fold 0 is the *best* baseline fold and
the *worst* leaking fold, so it understated the protocol effect by three points.
Single-fold diagnostics of protocol effects should not be trusted, including ours.

## 4. Discussion

**A single published scalar cannot serve as a comparison target here.** Depending
on which pair of conventions 84.1 encodes, our baseline is between 0.5 and 6.9
points short — a range wider than the 5.5-point spread across fourteen backbones
that the literature on this dataset reports `[CITE]`. The convention is not
usually stated, so the interval cannot be narrowed from the published record. The
honest consequence is that ranking methods across papers on this benchmark is not
currently possible, and that within-paper comparisons under a stated protocol are
the only ones that carry information.

**What we are not claiming.** We do not claim any specific published result used a
leaking split; we did not audit anyone's code. We claim that the conventions we
measured are sufficient to produce the reported range from an ordinary U-Net, that
they are rarely stated, and that a reader cannot distinguish the cases.

**Recommendation.** Report the split unit (patient or slice) and the aggregation
convention explicitly; report patient-averaged Dice alongside slice-averaged,
since per-patient slice counts here run from 1 to 38; and treat a cross-validated
σ as uninformative unless the folds sample patients.

## 5. Limitations

One dataset, one architecture, one seed per fold, five folds. The two protocols
are compared unpaired, for the reason given in §3.1. The residual 0.5 points is
unattributed and within what a different seed or a slightly different
architecture could produce. Our reconstruction of the published protocol is
inference from the reported numbers, not from published code.

## 6. Conclusion

A 6.9-point Dice gap on a standard benchmark turned out to be 1.3 points of
aggregation convention and 5.1 points of split protocol, with 0.5 unexplained and
nothing attributable to the model. The variance collapse is the part that should
change practice: a leaking cross-validated error bar is not a smaller error bar,
it is a different quantity.
